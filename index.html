From data to decisions

10x more company filings are available than five years ago. Yet teams still piece together MCA filings, credit reports and news alerts by hand every morning. This case study asks where the real value is — and what could be built to capture it.

Start reading LinkedIn ≈ 8 min read · 7 chapters
The problem

Everyone now has the same data. What's hard is deciding what matters and what to do next.

The bet

Build a prioritisation layer on top of existing data — explainable, source-linked, one click from action.

The plan

Start with Sales & BD teams, prove value in a 90-day pilot, then expand to credit, risk and research.

Six data types, one goal

Company, financial, ownership, regulatory, risk and market data all feed the same thing: a better business decision.

Go deeper: sizing the opportunity

TAM — the India RegTech market above. SAM — corporate-intelligence buyers: banks, NBFCs, FinTechs, insurers, PE/VC, enterprise procurement, consulting and legal teams. SOM — company-specific; it depends on deal size, win rate and sales capacity, which no player discloses, so it would be estimated bottom-up.

Who competes, and on what

The market splits into three tiers: broad corporate-data platforms, startup and private-market intelligence, and KYC/KYB workflow tools that win by sitting inside existing processes.

Positioning reflects stated product focus, not measured market share.

Why the top-right is still open

Nobody combines deep data with deep workflow integration.

Incumbents earn money per API call or bulk export, which rewards handing over more raw data — not building a filtered layer that gives users less to sift through.

Option A

Keep expanding the data catalogue

StrengthsBuilds on what exists, wider coverage, little disruption.
LimitationsRising data costs, data commoditises, exposed to generic AI search.
Option B · Chosen

Build a workflow intelligence layer

StrengthsCloser to the customer's workflow, stronger differentiation, higher switching costs, value-based pricing.
LimitationsNeeds deeper workflow understanding, more integration, and strong trust and explainability.

Why Sales & BD first

Better prospecting leads straight to better prioritisation, more focused effort and a result you can measure — pipeline. Credit, risk and research come next, once the pilot proves value.

The buyers behind these users — banks, NBFCs, FinTechs, insurers, PE/VC firms and enterprises — each buy for a different decision. That's why this project segments by decision, not job title.

Illustrative concept, not a built product.

Feature 1

Explainable match scores

Every ranking criterion is visible and traces back to the source entries behind it — MCA filings, GST records, registry entries.

Feature 2

Primary-source citations

Links open the original filing. If a claim can't be traced to a source, it isn't shown as fact.

Feature 3

One-click actions

Export a lead list or start outreach from the ranked view — decision and next step in one place.

Go deeper: why explainability is non-negotiable

Compliance buyers need to know why, not just what. Confidence is shown as a score rather than hidden behind confident-sounding language. RBI's June 2026 draft guidance on Model Risk Management already proposes a mandatory "kill switch" and board-level accountability for AI-driven decisions at banks and NBFCs — so the product must be built for that from day one.

Chapter 7 of 7

Measure decisions, not logins — and name the risks up front

In short

The north star is time to qualified opportunity. The biggest risk is trust, so the reasoning must always be visible.

North star metric

Time to Qualified Opportunity

How long it takes a user to go from starting a search to finding an opportunity worth pursuing. To be validated in the pilot.

Logins and searches show usage, not value. So success is tracked at three levels: adoption (repeat use), workflow impact (time to shortlist, steps removed) and business impact (qualified opportunities, conversion, pipeline).

Five risks and how to handle them

TrustWill users act on it?

Show why an opportunity ranks high, not just that it does, so users can sanity-check it.

Data qualityCan it be trusted?

Bad inputs produce confident bad outputs. Prioritise freshness, source transparency and verifiability.

HabitWill people switch?

Embed into existing tools instead of asking users to visit a new destination.

Over-automationWill users resist it?

Position it as decision support. The human stays accountable — as RBI's draft guidance requires.

CompetitionWill others close the gap?

Workflow tools may add intelligence and vice versa. The moat must be the decision layer, not the data.

Where this could lead

Data provider→Intelligence layer→Workflow partner→Enterprise decision infrastructure

After the pilot, the choice is simple: double down, change direction, or stop.
